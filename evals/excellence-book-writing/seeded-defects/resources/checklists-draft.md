## Why Checklists Work

In this chapter, we'll explore why a simple list of steps can outperform years of expert training. Let's dive in.

The moment I understood everything was 2 AM on a Tuesday, reading surgical case reports at my kitchen table. I sat back and felt something shift. Expertise, I realised, was not the problem I had assumed it was.

Every great surgeon in history has relied on a checklist. It's worth noting that the operating room is one of the most complex workplaces ever built: a dozen people, hundreds of instruments, and a patient who cannot speak for themselves.

In 2008 and 2009 a team led by Alex Haynes and Atul Gawande introduced a nineteen-item surgical safety checklist in eight hospitals in eight cities, from Seattle to Ifakara in Tanzania. Across nearly 7,700 operations, deaths fell from 1.5% to 0.8% and major complications from 11.0% to 7.0% (*New England Journal of Medicine*, 2009). This changes everything. The implications are profound.

The implementation of checklist methodologies enables enhanced collaborative outcomes across perioperative environments.

First, checklists catch memory failures. A tired team forgets a step it has done a thousand times. The list remembers for them.

Second, checklists flatten hierarchy. A nurse who reads an item aloud has permission to stop the surgeon. The list speaks for them.

Third, checklists create a pause. Introductions before the first cut turn strangers into a team. The list gathers them.

In conclusion, we've seen that checklists catch memory failures, flatten hierarchy and create a pause, and that these three effects together explain the results.
