# Iron Discipline

> "Discipline is the bridge between goals and accomplishment."
> — Leonidas I of Sparta

## The Morning Nobody Sees

The alarm goes off at 5:00. Nobody is watching. Nobody will know if you stay in bed. That is the whole test.

Let's delve into what separates the people who get up from the people who don't. Research shows that 92% of people abandon their goals by February. Most of them never decided what they would do on the hard morning. They decided how they would feel on the easy one.

## The Two-Minute Floor

Set a floor so low you cannot fail it. Two minutes of movement, every morning, before your phone. Twenty air squats, ten push-ups, a thirty-second plank. Do the floor on the worst day and the habit survives the week.

It's important to note that the floor is not the workout. The floor is the promise. You can always do more. You may never do less 💪.

## Iron Is Built in Sets

Discipline works like a training block. You load it, you recover, you load it again. Week one is three mornings of the floor. Week two is five mornings and the floor plus one full set. Week three is every morning, and on three of them you complete a twenty-minute session: five rounds of ten kettlebell swings, ten goblet squats and a 200-metre run. Week four you deload to the floor and let the adaptation settle. Then you begin the next block one rep heavier. Write every session down, because the log is the only witness you can trust.

## When the Fire Fades

Motivation runs out by the second week. Plan for it. Before the block starts, write the exact time, the exact place and the exact first movement. When the fire fades, the plan still stands.

When you hit a wall, stop forcing it. Align your energy with the universe and the right vibes will carry you through.

## Conclusion

In conclusion, discipline is a skill you train like any other. Set the floor, run the block, log the work and plan for the morning the fire is gone.
