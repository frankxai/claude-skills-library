# Page copy

## Contents

3. Rules per block: hero, proof, how it works, who it is for, what it costs, FAQ, CTA
4. Microcopy: buttons, forms, confirmation
5. Waitlist pages

## 1. Rules for every sentence

| Rule | Instead of | Write |
|---|---|---|
| One idea per sentence | "Shiftbook builds your rota and handles swaps, plus it syncs with payroll so you never miss a shift." | "Shiftbook builds your rota. Staff swap shifts in the app. Hours go to payroll." |
| Active voice | "Rotas are generated automatically." | "Shiftbook builds the rota." |
| Concrete nouns | "your scheduling needs" | "Friday's rota" |
| Numbers only with a source | "Saves hours every week" (no source) | "Owners in the beta cut rota time from 3 hours to 20 minutes a week" (ledger row) or no number |
| No superlatives | "the best rota app" | the fact that would make someone think so |
| The reader is "you" | "Users can export..." | "You can export..." |
| Say it once | the same benefit in the hero, the subhead and the first section | each block adds something new |

Also apply the humanizer's banned words, phrases and shapes. This file does not repeat them; see
step 6 in `SKILL.md`.

## 2. Page anatomy

The default order for a product or landing page. Drop any block you have nothing true to put in.

```text
1. Hero            what it is, who it is for, the one action
2. Proof           why a stranger should believe it
3. How it works    three to five steps the buyer actually takes
4. Who it is for   and who it is not for
5. What it costs   the price, or the honest reason there isn't one yet
6. FAQ             the objections the declined buyer raised
7. CTA             the action again, with what happens next
```

On a short page (a waitlist, an email) the same order compresses: hero, one proof line, cost
status, CTA.

## 3. Rules per block

### Hero

- **Headline**: one of the two survivors from the rubric in `headlines.md`.
- **Subhead**: one or two sentences that add what the headline left out, usually the mechanism
  or the buyer. Do not restate the headline.
- **One primary action.** A second, quieter link is allowed ("See how it works").
- **The visual shows the product in use**: a real screen, the real output, the physical thing.
  See `aesthetics.md` on the templated hero.
- No logos, numbers or badges in the hero unless they are in the ledger.

### Proof

- Real, attributed quotes with name, role and organisation, used with permission.
- Logos only for organisations that agreed to be shown.
- Numbers with the ledger row visible on request (a footnote or "how we measured" link).
- If there is no proof yet, say what exists instead: "In beta with 9 cafés in Utrecht since
  October." If even that is not true, leave the block out.
- Never: invented testimonials, stock-photo faces, "trusted by thousands", award badges without a
  source.

### How it works

- Three to five numbered steps in the buyer's order of doing them, not the system architecture.
- Each step starts with a verb the buyer performs: "Add your staff", "Set opening hours", "Publish".
- One screenshot or diagram per step at most. A step that needs a paragraph is two steps.

### Who it is for

- Name the buyer specifically, with the boundary: "Cafés and small restaurants with 3 to 15 staff."
- Say who it is not for: "Not built for hospital rosters or union shift rules yet."
  This builds more trust than it costs in sign-ups.

### What it costs

- Show the price if it exists, with what is included and the billing unit.
- If there is no price yet, say so and say when that will be decided, only if a date is decided:
  "Free during the beta. Pricing is set before the beta ends; you will get 30 days' notice."
- Do not hide the price behind "Contact us" for a self-serve product.
- No struck-through "was" prices that never existed.

### FAQ

- Questions come from step 7 (the declined buyer) and from real support or sales conversations.
- Each answer starts with the answer: "Yes." "No." "About 10 minutes." Then the detail.
- Include the uncomfortable ones: "What happens to my data if you shut down?", "Can I cancel?"
- Five to eight questions. More than that is a docs page.

### CTA

- The button names the action: "Join the waitlist", "Start the free trial", "Book a 20-minute call".
- Under the button, the next thing that happens: "We email you once, when your invite is ready."
  "No card needed. The trial ends on its own after 14 days."
- Repeat the hero action, not a new one.
- Never: "Submit", "Learn more" as the primary action, "Get started" with no hint of what starts.

## 4. Microcopy

- **Buttons**: verb plus object, sentence case: "Save rota", "Send invite".
- **Form labels**: above the field, plain: "Work email". Placeholder text is not a label.
- **Ask for the minimum.** A waitlist needs an email. Every extra field needs a reason you can
  state, and should be optional.
- **Errors** say what happened and what to do: "That email is missing an @. Check and try again."
- **Confirmation** says what happens next and when: "You're on the list. We'll email
  you@cafe.nl when invites open. No other emails."
- **Consent** is a separate, unticked checkbox if you want to send anything beyond the stated
  emails.

## 5. Waitlist pages

The waitlist comes before any checkout (hard rule 4 in `SKILL.md`).

- State what the reader is joining: a beta, early access, a launch notification.
- State what they will receive and how often.
- If places are limited, give the real number and the reason ("20 cafés, because we onboard each
  one by hand"). No seeded counters, no "only 3 spots left" unless it is true and current.
- No countdown timers. No launch date unless the date is decided.
- One field (email), one button, one sentence of what happens next.
- A waitlist count shown publicly is the real, current count or it is not shown.
