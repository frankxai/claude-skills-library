# Movement Architect

A brand is remembered. A movement is joined. The difference is the unit of work: a brand ships
messages, a movement gives people a new identity, a cause they can repeat in their own words,
and a vehicle that gets them somewhere the old one could not. Everything commercial (the lead
magnet, the ladder, the membership) hangs off that core, so this skill builds the core first
and the funnel second. Done the other way round you get a funnel nobody wants to be seen in.

The method is assembled from people who built real movements and real ladders, each used for
what they are good at and checked against their documented blind spots. The canon, with
credits and caveats, is in `references/frameworks.md`. Read it the first time you run this
skill; after that the procedure below is enough.

## When to use this

Someone has a brand, a product idea, a community, or a vague "this should be huge" and needs
it turned into a movement with a working ladder underneath: who leads it, what it stands for,
what the new opportunity is, what is free, what is paid, how the first thing is delivered, and
how success is reported honestly. Also use it to diagnose a funnel or launch that is not
converting; nine times out of ten the fault is a sophistication or awareness mismatch, not the
button colour.

Do not use it for single-asset copywriting (one email, one ad) with the movement already
defined, or for pricing a B2B enterprise contract. Hand those to the relevant specialist.

## The shape of a movement

Eric Hoffer described mass movements in *The True Believer* (1951); Russell Brunson turned the
pattern into a builder's checklist in *Expert Secrets* (2017). Three parts, plus two the
practice keeps adding:

| Part | What it is | Test |
|---|---|---|
| Leader | A person (the "attractive character"), not a logo. Backstory, flaws, parables, a clear polarity. | Can a stranger describe the leader in one sentence after one page? |
| Future-based cause | Where the group is going, stated as a future state, not a feature list. Comes with an identity label members use for themselves. | Would a member put the label in their bio? |
| New opportunity | A new vehicle, not an improvement of the old one. People do not want a better version of the thing that failed them. | Does the pitch name the old vehicle it replaces? |
| Rituals and language | The repeated words, formats and small ceremonies that make members recognisable to each other. | Could you spot a member from their vocabulary alone? |
| Proof inside, not on top | Receipts, results and mechanism live inside the pages. The headline carries identity and feeling. | Does the headline sell who you become, with proof one scroll below? |

The enemy of a movement is the old vehicle, never other people. Position against the way
things used to be done, and name named competitors only in honest comparison tables.

## Procedure

Work the steps in order. Each produces a block of the Movement Brief
(`references/movement-brief.md`); skipping one leaves a hole the brief will show.

### 1. Read the market's own words first

Eugene Schwartz's rule: copy cannot create desire, it channels desire that already exists, so
the market writes the ad. Before any framing, collect the market's language. Mine reviews,
forum threads, competitor sales pages, support complaints, and the exact phrases buyers use
for the pain and the dream. Keep a swipe log with source and date.

Then place the market on two scales, and keep them apart:

- **Sophistication** (how many times the whole market has heard the claim): stage 1 state the
  claim; stage 2 enlarge it; stage 3 introduce a new mechanism; stage 4 elaborate the
  mechanism; stage 5 shift to identification. AI tooling and creator markets in 2026 usually
  sit at stage 4 or 5: claims are exhausted, mechanisms are everywhere, so identity is what
  is left to sell. Check it against your competitor table rather than assuming it; when it
  holds, it is exactly why a movement outperforms a product page.
- **Awareness** (where one prospect is right now): unaware, problem-aware, solution-aware,
  product-aware, most aware. Every asset later gets tagged with the stage it meets.

Write a competitor-language table: for the five closest alternatives, their headline claim,
their named mechanism, the identity they offer, and their price. The gaps in that table are
your positioning options.

### 2. Choose the buyer and the bar

April Dunford's chain: competitive alternatives (including "do nothing" and "ask ChatGPT")
→ what you uniquely do → the value that enables → who cares most → the market frame that
makes it obvious. Start from the people who already love the thing, not from the people you
wish would. Seth Godin's smallest viable audience is the same instruction from the other
side: pick the smallest group you can delight so completely that they tell others.

Name three current, best-in-class references this must beat, each on one stated dimension.
"Comparable" is a failing verdict. Open them this week; do not quote them from memory.

### 3. Build the movement core

Fill the five rows of the shape table for this brand. Rules that save rework:

- The leader is a human with a backstory and an "epiphany bridge": the moment they saw the
  new opportunity. Tell that story the way it happened, so the listener has the epiphany
  rather than being argued into it. Flaws and polarity stay in; sanded-down founders repel.
- The cause is future-based and bigger than the product. Write it as a one-line future state
  plus a slogan members can say. Then write the identity label (what members call
  themselves). If the label embarrasses you, it will embarrass them.
- The new opportunity names the old vehicle it replaces and why the old one cannot get there.
  Improvement offers ("better, faster, cheaper") lose to opportunity offers ("a different
  way") in sophisticated markets.
- Rituals: one recurring format (a weekly letter, a live build, a monthly ship list), one
  piece of shared vocabulary, one visible badge of membership.

### 4. Construct the offer

Alex Hormozi's value equation: Value = (Dream Outcome × Perceived Likelihood) ÷ (Time Delay ×
Effort). Raise the top line with specificity and proof; cut the bottom line with templates,
done-for-you pieces and fast first wins. Stack the offer until the obvious objections are
each answered by a component. Price on value, never on cost, and never discount; discounts
need an anchor price you have not earned yet.

Risk reversal must be true. A guarantee you cannot honour, a count you seeded, or a timer
that resets costs the movement permanently, because movements run on trust in the leader.

### 5. Lay the value ladder

Brunson's ladder, with the economics Hormozi makes explicit: the front end acquires, the back
end profits, continuity is the linchpin.

| Rung | Job | Typical shape |
|---|---|---|
| Free | Prove the new opportunity works, in the member's own hands, inside minutes | open-source core, a skill, a template, a calculator, a chapter |
| Entry | First money, first result, funds acquisition | a focused pack, a short guide, a single template bundle |
| Core | The full method, outcome-complete | a playbook, a course, a boilerplate with docs |
| Continuity | The room, the updates, the cadence | membership with perks, updates, live rituals |
| Premium | Depth or access for the few who want it | cohort, all-access, implementation |

Rules: every rung's gift is a carved-out slice of the real product, never a brochure. Nothing
shows a checkout until it is actually finished and checked; until then the rung is a waitlist
that asks what people would pay. One repo, one line, one call to action per surface.

### 6. Build the lead magnet

Follow `references/lead-magnet-builder.md`. Short version: pick the archetype that matches the
prospect's awareness stage (a quiz or calculator for problem-aware traffic, a template or
slice of product for solution-aware, a comparison or receipts page for product-aware), promise
one specific outcome, deliver it inside sixty seconds of opt-in, and capture in two steps so
the email is never blocked by the questions. Then ask the three questions that turn a list
into evidence: expected price band, who they are, what they are trying to do.

### 7. Package into distributable products

Follow `references/product-ladder.md` for shapes, marketplaces and the packaging checklist per
shape. Every product is versioned, installable or openable by a stranger without the leader
in the room, and carries one README and one call to action. Publish state is measured (a live
listing, an install command that works), never claimed.

### 8. Sequence the launch

Order content by awareness stage: identity and story for the unaware and problem-aware,
mechanism for the solution-aware, offer and proof for the product-aware. Diagnose every
underperforming asset with Hook, Story, Offer; one of the three is always the broken one.
Borrow audiences before you buy them: list the places your buyers already gather (Chet
Holmes's Dream 100, popularised by Brunson) and work in before you pay in. Volume before
optimisation: a fixed daily output for a fixed run beats a perfect single launch.

### 9. Report success honestly

Movement metrics are real counts of real people: members by identity label, installs that
work, would-pay signals by price band, opt-in rate against your own baseline (not a blog's
average), receipts delivered, retention of the ritual. Never publish a count below the
threshold where it looks impressive; silence beats "join 12 others". Report what you measured,
how, and when, next to what you did not measure.

## Output: the Movement Brief

Produce one document with these blocks, in this order, and nothing else as a deliverable:

1. Market reality (sophistication stage, awareness map, competitor-language table, swipe log)
2. Buyer and bar (best-fit customer, alternatives, three references to beat)
3. Movement core (leader, cause and label, new opportunity and old vehicle, rituals, proof plan)
4. Offer (value equation worked, stack, price hypothesis, honest risk reversal)
5. Ladder (five rungs with the gift, the shape and the gate state of each)
6. Lead magnet (archetype, promise, delivery, capture, three questions)
7. Packaging (shape, marketplace, install or open path, version)
8. Launch sequence (assets by awareness stage, Dream 100 list, daily volume)
9. Scoreboard (what is measured, how, by whom; what is not yet measured)
10. Open rulings (the decisions only the founder can take)

Template with field notes: `references/movement-brief.md`.

## Honesty rails

These are what separate a movement from a launch-bro funnel. A brief that trips two rows is
not finished.

| Tell | Fix |
|---|---|
| Invented or secondary-sourced numbers ("trusted by 10,000", a founder's revenue claim) | measured count with method and date, or cut |
| Seeded scarcity: fake counts, undecided deadlines, resetting timers | real window (founding cohort closes at launch), real positions, or silence |
| Discount with no earned anchor price | price lock, named credit, lifetime access instead |
| Improvement pitch in a stage 4 or 5 market | new opportunity with a named old vehicle |
| Proof-led headline in an identity market | identity and feeling in the headline, proof one scroll down |
| Vocabulary from nowhere: "unleash", "seamless", "10x", "revolutionary" | the buyer's own noun, the measured number, the verb the product does |
| Enemy is a person or a company | the enemy is the old vehicle |
| Hero → three cards → testimonial carousel with no testimonials | one composition for this content; delete sections with nothing true to say |

## Worked example, in brief

A solo founder who runs a fleet of coding agents wants to sell to other builders.

- Market: stage 5 (every AI tool claims speed and quality). Buyers speak of "babysitting",
  "slop", "it looked done", "I can't trust the output".
- Buyer: builders who already run two or more agents and are tired of being the only
  reviewer. Alternatives: do it by hand, a bigger prompt, another orchestrator.
- Core: leader is the founder who runs the fleet in public, flaws and failures included.
  Cause: "one person, a fleet, work that stands on its own." Label: operators. New
  opportunity: the agent team as the unit of work, replacing the single chat window. Ritual: a
  weekly public ship list.
- Offer: outcome-complete playbooks with worked receipts, priced on value; guarantee is a
  real refund window.
- Ladder: open-source core (free) → first playbook (core) → membership with the room and
  updates as perks (continuity) → all-access.
- Lead magnet: the one skill from the playbook that saves an hour this week, installed in
  sixty seconds, with the three questions asked afterwards.
- Packaging: skill pack on GitHub, playbooks via a merchant of record with licence keys.
- Scoreboard: installs that run, would-pay by band, members who post, weekly ship list kept.
