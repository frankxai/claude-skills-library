# Lead magnet builder

A lead magnet is a complete solution to a narrow problem, free, that reveals the bigger
problem the core offer solves (Hormozi's definition) and is a carved-out slice of the real
product (the rule that keeps it from being a brochure). It is the first rung of the ladder
and the first proof that the new opportunity works in the member's own hands.

## 1. Pick the archetype by awareness stage

| Prospect is | Best archetypes | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Unaware | manifesto, story, a surprising measured result | they need a reason to care before a tool |
| Problem-aware | quiz or diagnostic, calculator, scorecard | names their problem back to them with a number |
| Solution-aware | template, checklist, cheat sheet, mini course (3 to 5 emails), one skill or script | lets them try the new vehicle in minutes |
| Product-aware | slice of the product, comparison table, receipts page, free chapter | shows the thing itself and how it differs |
| Most aware | founding window, waitlist with price question, early access | they are deciding, not learning |

Quizzes and calculators post the highest opt-in rates in published data, but note the
denominator: many reports count people who already started the quiz, not everyone who landed.

## 2. Promise one outcome

Write the promise as: *[specific outcome] in [time] without [the thing they hate]*. One
outcome. If you need "and", you have two magnets. The outcome must be true for the first
person who tries it today, not for the median user after a month.

## 3. Build it from the product, not beside it

Take the slice from the real product: the one skill from the pack, chapter two of the book,
the intake template from the course, the scoring script from the playbook. A slice proves the
whole; a brochure proves nothing. Keep it current by regenerating it from the product on each
release rather than maintaining a separate artefact.

## 4. Deliver inside sixty seconds

The gift lands in the first email or on the thank-you page immediately. Delay converts a
gift into a transaction. If the magnet is installable (a skill, a template, a repo), the
install command sits on the confirmation page and in the email, and it works without the
leader in the room.

## 5. Capture in two steps

Step one asks for the email only. Step two, after they are in, asks the three questions that
turn a list into evidence. Each is skippable:

1. **Expected price** for the full thing, as a band (for example under 50, 50 to 150, 150 to
   350, more). This is the only price evidence a pre-launch product has.
2. **Who they are**, as a role they would recognise.
3. **What they are trying to do**, free text, one line.

Store every signal in one schema across every brand you run, with a product identifier on the
row, so cross-brand demand can be read later. A mailto link, a generic form service, or an
audience list with no product identifier is a bespoke capture even when it renders as a
working form; it beats a dead button this week and gets migrated next week.

## 6. Honest scarcity only

Scarcity is the window (a founding cohort that closes at launch and never reopens), never the
units of an infinite digital good. Publish real counts only above the threshold where they
help; below it, say nothing. Offer status and permanence (price lock, named credit, input
rights, lifetime access), never a discount, because a discount needs an anchor price nobody
has paid yet. A milestone announced with no date, or a count reached with no promise kept, is
a defect of the highest severity.

## 7. Benchmarks, and why not to trust them

Published figures disagree by an order of magnitude because they define the visitor, the
offer and the traffic source differently. Use them only to sanity-check, then beat your own
baseline.

| Figure | Source (read 2026-11-03) | Caveat |
|---|---|---|
| ~6.6% median landing page conversion, all industries | Unbounce analysis of ~41,000 pages (Q4 2024), via Leadpages 2026 benchmark post | mixes opt-in pages with demo and purchase pages |
| ~19.3% for email-sourced traffic, ~11.3% paid search | same dataset, via Digital Applied summary | channel medians, not opt-in specific |
| ~23% average for sign-up optimised pages | ClickMinded 2026 benchmark | vendor dataset |
| 2% to 8% for a decent cold-traffic magnet page | Lilach Bullock | practitioner estimate |
| 40%+ opt-in on quizzes | Interact, ~2,100 live quizzes | denominator often excludes bounces before the quiz |

Report your own rate with the denominator stated (all landings, or engaged visitors) and the
traffic source. A number without those two facts is decoration.

## 8. Anti-patterns

- A PDF that summarises the sales page.
- A magnet for a different buyer than the core offer (the list grows, nobody buys).
- Gating the gift behind the three questions.
- A count below threshold ("join 12 others").
- A timer that resets.
- Promising the outcome of the paid product in the free one.

## 9. Build checklist

- [ ] Archetype matches the awareness stage of the traffic it will meet
- [ ] One promise, true today for the first user
- [ ] Sliced from the real product, regenerable on release
- [ ] Delivered in under sixty seconds, install or open path tested by a stranger
- [ ] Two-step capture, three skippable questions, one schema with product identifier
- [ ] No seeded counts, no undecided dates, no resetting timers, no discounts
- [ ] Baseline opt-in rate recorded with denominator and traffic source before any claim
