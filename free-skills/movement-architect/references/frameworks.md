# The canon: who the method borrows from, for what, and where each is wrong

Use a framework for the job it was built for. Every entry lists its blind spot because the
blind spots are where movements turn into infomercials. Dates are publication years. Claims
marked *secondary* come from summaries or press, not the primary text; verify before quoting.

## Movement builders

**Eric Hoffer, *The True Believer* (1951).** The original anatomy of mass movements: the
frustrated who want to escape a spoiled self, the appeal of a future over the present, the
role of a devil, the unifying power of ritual and doctrine. Use it to understand *why* the
three-part pattern works. Blind spot: written about political and religious movements; the
darker mechanics (fanaticism, a scapegoat) are exactly what a commercial movement must refuse.
Hence this skill's rule that the enemy is the old vehicle, never people.

**Russell Brunson, *Expert Secrets* (2017; rev. 2020).** Three parts of a movement: the
charismatic leader (attractive character), the future-based cause, the new opportunity.
Brunson credits Hoffer. Also: the epiphany bridge (tell the origin story so the listener has
the aha), the Big Domino belief, false-belief patterns (vehicle, internal, external), the
Stack (credited to Armand Morin). *DotCom Secrets* (2015): the value ladder. *Traffic Secrets*
(2020): Hook, Story, Offer; Dream 100 (created by Chet Holmes, *The Ultimate Sales Machine*,
2007). Blind spot: funnel maximalism and aggressive upsell patterns read badly in trust-driven
and brand-sensitive markets; his revenue milestones are marketing claims; every road leads to
his own software. Take the architecture, leave the pressure tactics.

**Seth Godin, *Tribes* (2008), *This Is Marketing* (2018), *Permission Marketing* (1999).**
"People like us do things like this." Smallest viable audience. Marketing as something you do
*for* people with their permission. Use for who-is-it-for and for the ethics of attention.
Blind spot: inspirational, not operational; little on pricing or sequencing.

**Kevin Kelly, "1,000 True Fans" (2008 essay).** A creator needs a thousand people who will
buy anything you make, not a million followers. The economic floor of a movement. Blind spot:
the essay predates platform fees and discovery costs; Kelly himself later noted the number
moves with price and margin.

## Offer and ladder

**Alex Hormozi, *$100M Offers* (2021).** Value = (Dream Outcome × Perceived Likelihood of
Achievement) ÷ (Time Delay × Effort and Sacrifice). The Grand Slam Offer: starving crowd,
stacked value, premium price, risk reversal. Leverage order: market > offer > persuasion.
*$100M Leads* (2023): the Core Four (warm outreach, free content, cold outreach, paid ads),
Rule of 100 (a hundred actions a day for a hundred days), lead magnets as a complete solution
to a narrow problem that reveals the bigger problem the core offer solves, lead getters.
*$100M Money Models* (2025): sequence offers so one customer funds the next two. Blind spot:
engineered urgency and bonus stacks read infomercial in brand-sensitive contexts; his own
figures are self-reported.

**Tim Ferriss, *The 4-Hour Workweek* (2007).** Test the offer before building it (ads to a
page, measure response), the "muse" (a low-maintenance product business), 80/20 on customers
and tasks. Use for validation before build. Blind spot: the lifestyle framing ages badly; the
validation mechanics still hold.

**Pat Flynn, *Will It Fly?* (2016), *Superfans* (2019).** Validation through conversation and
pre-sales before building; the fan pyramid (casual → active → connected → superfan) and the
small moments that move people up it. Use for the ritual layer and for validation. Blind
spot: creator-scale; little on pricing or platform economics.

**Naval Ravikant, *The Almanack of Naval Ravikant* (2020, Eric Jorgenson).** Specific
knowledge, accountability, leverage (code and media are permissionless leverage), "productize
yourself", play long-term games with long-term people. Use for the founder's leverage model
and for why a one-person fleet can be a movement. Blind spot: aphoristic; nothing on
execution or distribution.

## Market and positioning

**Eugene Schwartz, *Breakthrough Advertising* (1966).** Five stages of awareness (unaware,
problem, solution, product, most aware) and five stages of market sophistication (state the
claim, enlarge it, new mechanism, elaborate the mechanism, identification). Copy channels
existing desire; copy is assembled from the market's own words. Keep awareness (one prospect)
and sophistication (the whole market) apart. Blind spot: single-ad, print, direct-response
frame; nothing on community or brand over time.

**April Dunford, *Obviously Awesome* (2019; 2nd ed. 2026), *Sales Pitch* (2023).** The
positioning chain: competitive alternatives → unique attributes → value → who cares most →
market category (→ trend). Start from best-fit customers. Category creation is overrated;
the status quo is the real competitor. Blind spot: B2B-tech derived; qualitative.

**Geoffrey Moore, *Crossing the Chasm* (1991).** Visionaries buy a change; pragmatists buy
references and a whole product. A movement recruits visionaries; the ladder must eventually
serve pragmatists with complete products. Blind spot: enterprise software framing.

**Byron Sharp, *How Brands Grow* (2010).** Penetration beats loyalty; distinctive assets
beat differentiation claims; buyers barely perceive differences. The useful corrective to
niche-first thinking: your rituals and visual assets must be distinctive and repeated. Blind
spot: mass consumer goods data; thin on creator and software markets.

## Founders and the current vibe

**Steve Jobs.** Keynote craft: one headline per product ("1,000 songs in your pocket"), the
rule of three, the "one more thing" reveal, say no to a thousand things. Use for the cause
line and the launch sequence. *Secondary*: most of this is observed from recorded keynotes
and Isaacson's biography (2011), not a method he wrote down.

**Elon Musk.** Mission as cause (a multi-planetary species, sustainable energy), reasoning
from first principles, building in public with failures shown. Use for how a future-based
cause pulls a workforce and a customer base at once. Blind spot: the polarity cuts both ways;
*secondary* sourcing for most quotes.

**Sam Altman.** *Secondary*: widely reported prediction of a ten-person, then a one-person,
billion-dollar company; "The Intelligence Age" essay (2024). Use as the macro narrative a
founder-plus-fleet movement can ride, and verify the primary quote before publishing it.

**Dario Amodei, "Machines of Loving Grace" (October 2024 essay).** A concrete, optimistic
account of what powerful AI could do for health, poverty and governance. Use as the
future-based cause at civilisational scale, written by a lab leader rather than a marketer.
*Secondary*: press-reported odds about a one-person billion-dollar company; verify.

**The 2026 macro vibe (what is globally happening, as of this writing).** The "one person plus
agents" company is the dominant founder narrative: ministers, labs and venture blogs all
repeat it; the sceptical counter is redundancy and burnout (one person is a single point of
failure). Agent skills, prompt packs and templates are the fastest-growing digital product
categories by count; software development is the largest category by revenue on at least one
large creator marketplace (vendor data, directional). A movement built on "one founder, a
fleet, work that stands on its own" is riding the current, so its proof has to be real
because every competitor is making the same claim.

## Sciences behind it

**Robert Cialdini, *Influence* (1984; 2021 edition adds Unity).** Reciprocity, commitment and
consistency, social proof, authority, liking, scarcity, unity. Give → prove → position →
invite is reciprocity and commitment run in the honest order. Unity (shared identity) is the
movement principle.

**Jonah Berger, *Contagious* (2013).** STEPPS: social currency, triggers, emotion, public,
practical value, stories. The identity label and badge are "public"; the ritual is a
"trigger".

**BJ Fogg, Fogg Behavior Model.** Behaviour = Motivation × Ability × Prompt. A lead magnet
delivered in sixty seconds is an ability fix; a weekly ritual is a prompt.

**Daniel Kahneman, *Thinking, Fast and Slow* (2011).** Loss aversion and anchoring. Why a
seeded anchor price is both dishonest and fragile, and why a real founding window works.

**Clayton Christensen, jobs to be done.** People hire a product to make progress in a
situation. The "new opportunity" is a new way to do the job, which is why improvements lose.

## How to read competitor language (Schwartz, operationalised)

1. Collect ten to twenty sources: sales pages, reviews (one to three stars are the richest),
   community threads, support docs, the competitor's own changelog.
2. Log every recurring claim, mechanism name, identity word and price, with source and date.
3. Count how often each claim appears. Three or more competitors making the same claim means
   the market is past stage 2 for that claim; do not make it again.
4. Find the mechanism nobody has named, or the identity nobody is offering. That is the gap.
5. Write your headline in the buyer's words from the log, not yours. Keep the log in the
   brief so the next person can see where the words came from.
